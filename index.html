Critical AI Ethics & Cautions

  • 1 No Sensitive Data: Do not input Protect Health Information (PHI), proprietary clinical data, or unpatented Intellectual Property (IP) into public AI models.
  • 2 Hallucination Risk: Beware of AI hallucinations. Generative models will confidently fabricate citations, invent non-existent assays, and overestimate institutional feasibility.
  • 3 The "Clay" Rule: AI generates the raw clay. The researcher must sculpt, refine, and validate it against institutional reality, scientific rigor, and personal expertise. You are entirely responsible for the final submission.

General Grant-Writing Tips

Evidence-based strategies synthesized from the literature to maximize your funding success.

Know Your Audience (The Reviewer)

Reviewers are busy researchers reading many applications, often outside their exact niche. The application is for the reviewer, not you.

  • • Write in clear, concise language using the active voice.
  • • Avoid jargon and ensure formatting is highly organized with clear topic sentences (NIH).
  • • Don't make the reviewer work hard to find your gap or impact (Araj et al.).

Feasibility & Scope

One of the most common errors is proposing an overly ambitious amount of work that cannot be completed in the award duration.

  • • Never propose interdependent "domino aims" where Aim 2 relies entirely on the success of Aim 1 (Guyer et al.).
  • • Provide sufficient preliminary data to establish competence and feasibility.
  • • Rigorously discuss potential pitfalls and alternative approaches (Araj et al.).

Tell a Cohesive Story

People comprehend best in stories. Present your research as a cohesive narrative that leaves no puzzling gaps in logic.

  • • Define the exact problem, the critical gap, and the "hook" that justifies why this research is needed right now.
  • • Emphasize significance and innovation by showing how this changes patient care or breaks a scientific impasse (Guyer et al.).

Iterate & Enlist Help

A competitive application requires extensive iterative feedback. Poorly-constructed proposals hastily prepared inevitably stand out.

  • • Seek brutal, constructive criticism early from funded investigators, statistical experts, and mentors (Guyer et al.).
  • • Start by solidifying the Specific Aims page before writing the full approach (Zlowodzki et al.).

General Grounded AI Tips

Mastering generative AI for grants requires more than generic queries. Use these advanced methodologies to produce rigorous and verifiable drafts.

1. RAG Approaches

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG): Do not rely on the AI's baseline training data for scientific literature. Upload verifiable PDFs (e.g., successful past grants, core agency guidelines, primary literature) in grounding AI tools like NotebookLM or custom tools (e.g., GPTs, Gems, Skills). This grounds the AI in reality and drastically reduces hallucinations.

2. Custom Tools & Personas

Configure system instructions to make the AI adopt specific personas (e.g., "Grumpy NIH Study Section Reviewer" or "Clinical Trials Methodologist"). Mandate that the AI output adheres strictly to specific agency formatting (e.g., DOW CDMRP vs. NIH R01) and explicitly ban generic academic fluff.

3. Meta-Prompting

Use the AI to write the prompt for you. Tell the AI your end goal (e.g., "I need to write a Specific Aims page for a clinical trial on a novel PET tracer") and ask it what information, context, and constraints it needs from you to produce the best possible draft.

Module 1 Significance and Hypotheses

Module Education & Task

You will draft a concise 5-sentence significance/decision-impact paragraph that explicitly outlines the hard clinical/scientific decision faced, the current limitations, the specific knowledge gap, and exactly what your proposal delivers to change practice or future research. You will also develop 2-3 research hypotheses.

Tips for Success

  • The "Why Now" Factor: Reviewers look for timeliness. Explicitly state why this gap hasn't been solved yet and what advance makes it solvable now.
  • Sponsor-Fit Tone Tuning: A DoW hook emphasizes military relevance; an NIH hook emphasizes mechanistic discovery. Tailor your tone deliberately.

AI Tips

  • Strategic Research Gaps: Prompt the AI with your broad topic of interest and ask it to brainstorm and pinpoint specific, strategic research gaps or unmet clinical needs.
  • Concise Literature Summaries: Use grounded AI tools to synthesize your reference PDFs into a concise, high-level summary of the current state of the evidence or use toools like OpenEvidence or Perplexity to obtain a summary.
  • Critique & Tune Tone: Paste your drafted significance statement into the AI and ask it to critique the text, ensuring the language and tone are optimally tuned for your target sponsor's mission.

Peer & Expert Feedback Reminder

Exchange drafts with a partner. Roleplay as the "Skeptic": flag feasibility issues, confounders, and check the key sources of bias in their significance framing. Engage the experts at your table to perform a reality check on your Sponsor-Fit language.

Module 2 Specific Aims and Study Endpoints

Module Education & Task

In this module, you will translate your rough idea into a structured, one-page Specific Aims document. Building on your significance narrative from Module 1, craft 2-4 narrrowly focused Specific Aims. Focus on clear and achievable endpoints within the funding timeframe.

Tips for Success

  • Avoid Domino Aims: Ensure Aim 1 is foundational but not co-dependent on Aim 2. If Aim 1 fails, Aim 2 must still be executable.
  • Use Actionable Verbs: Avoid aims that seek to "describe" or "explore." Use measurable, definitive verbs like "quantify," "determine," or "validate."

AI Tips

  • Action Verbs: Use AI tools to brainstorm and propose strong, definitive action verbs for your specific aims.
  • Endpoint Achievability: Ask the AI to critique and clarify your endpoints to ensure they are realistic and achievable during the proposed timeframe.
  • Reviewer Clarity: Prompt the AI to evaluate your aims from the perspective of an external reviewer to ensure they are completely clear and logically structured.

Peer & Expert Feedback Reminder

Share your generated Specific Aims draft with your table peers for a "Micro Study Section" review. Utilize the experts at your table during the "Aims Triage" round to identify your biggest reviewer worry and receive one actionable fix.

Module 3 Methodology, Approach Map, and Risk Mitigation

Module Education & Task

The methodological approach is the capstone of the grant. You will build a one-page approach backbone covering study design, population, endpoints, and analysis. Following this, you will construct a Risk Mitigation Matrix to identify the top 3 scientific or logistical risks.

Tips for Success

  • Methodological Rigor: Clearly justify your study design, sample size, and statistical methods to ensure high scientific rigor and reproducibility.
  • Proactive De-risking: Do not assume everything will work perfectly. You must list alternative strategies and pitfalls for every aim.
  • Go/No-Go Logic: Incorporate clear, quantitative thresholds for success (e.g., "If recruitment falls below X/month, we will open site Y") to prove feasibility.

AI Tips

  • Novel Methods: Utilize AI tools to propose new research or statistical methods that you have not used before.
  • Rigor Critique: Use AI tools to critique the rigor of your methods.
  • Risk Mitigation Matrix: Use AI tools to craft a Risk Mitigation Matrix for your research, including identifying the probability, impact, risk score, and mitigation strategies.

Peer & Expert Feedback Reminder

Subject your Approach Backbone to a "Fatal Flaw" stress test with your table. Let peers actively search for missing comparators, flawed endpoints, or unaddressed risks. Work with the experts at your table to ensure your alternatives are highly specific and realistic.

Post-Workshop Action Plan

What is your next step? Make a commitment for a discrete action based on what you learned or the feedback you received in this workshop. Please write your Action Plan down in your Google Doc workspace or personal notes before you leave.

Example 7-Day Action Plans:

  • • Revise: Edit one of the items written today (Significance, Specific Aims, or Methodology) based on peer/expert critiques.
  • • Distribute: Identify 2-3 peer reviewers at your home institution to send your Specific Aims to.
  • • Research: Review the grant requirements on your target sponsor/funding pages more closely.
  • • Tailor: Refine your written content to specifically align with a particular funding agency's mission.
